<design_contract>
The single new table stores discriminated entity-head rows and effect-unit rows; define explicit CHECK constraints for the two row kinds. A head has a unique tenant/family/entity identity and a server-created current request revision. Effect uniqueness is tenant/family/entity/request revision/logical unit, independent of job id and lease generation. A reserved typed head identity must not collide with a caller's unit key. The revision is durable, not a timestamp or caller-provided authority token. Finance uses head arbitration only.
An effect records immutable normalized-payload hash, actual resolved provider endpoint identity/model, normalization/chunking version and result shape/dimensions; never persist API keys, raw source text or full request headers in a receipt. Pin actual provider selection and the transmitted truncated/normalized texts before admission. A prepared in-memory request must not let public callers substitute a different hash/model after admission.
States distinguish admitted, received and committed; an explicit indeterminate disposition may accompany an admitted row after ambiguity. Persist validated response vectors per bounded existing batch (at most 64 inputs) as received under current ownership. This permits reuse of earlier received batches without another paid call. Final domain replacement plus associated receipt commits and durable job progress are atomic. If a response arrives after ownership loss, do not grant it authority to update the receipt. A committed effect is reusable only for exactly the same revision/hash/configuration. Mismatch is a conflict, not overwrite or retry.
An explicit new user request advances the head revision atomically with domain reset and job enqueue. Duplicate jobs for that request reuse its revision. Old jobs with missing/old revision cannot silently attach themselves to a later explicit request. For legacy queued jobs, define and test one bounded adoption under head/domain locks; conflicting or ambiguous legacy work stops without a paid call. Corpus adoption must validate source fingerprints when selecting/reusing a head revision.
Use one lock order: the owned job row (when a running job is involved), then head, then domain rows/receipt units in stable order. Authorized user reset/remove paths lock head then domain and insert a new job if needed; they do not acquire an old running job row after the head. Head invalidation fences old workers without introducing that reverse lock order. The job runtime remains unchanged in this foundation.
The existing-transaction scope helper captures prior role and org/profile GUC values, sets app_ledger/current tenant/profile before domain or receipt SQL, and restores captured values after a successful scoped callback, including a non-SQL thrown error while the transaction remains usable. SQL failure aborts the transaction: preserve its original exception and never mask it with a failed restoration statement. Preserve the 20s transaction timeout policy and existing withOrgCore behavior. Do not grant app_ledger general bg_jobs privileges. Job cursor/enqueue work occurs after scope restoration but within the same outer transaction.
</design_contract>
